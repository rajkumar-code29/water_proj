## 2. How much dirty water goes in

| Measure | Value | Source / date | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kukatpally nala | 70 MLD | neliti review (academic) | Older study |
| Kukatpally nala | **155 MLD** | Telangana Today, 15 Dec 2020 | Reported |
| Balkapur nala | 13.3 MLD | neliti review | Older study |
| Banjara nala | 6 MLD | neliti review | Older study |
| Picket nala | 5.7 MLD | neliti review | Older study |
| **Total nala inflow, study basis** | **~95 MLD** (sum of the four) | neliti review | Older study |
| Total polluted inflow, reported | **~450 MLD** | The Federal, 18 Feb 2026 | Reported, method unknown |
| STP capacity around the lake | **55 MLD** (5 + 20 + 30) | Telangana Today, 2 Nov 2022 | Reported |
| Planned STP upgrade | 110 MLD (10 + 40 + 60), Hybrid Annuity Model | Telangana Today, 2 Nov 2022 | Status not confirmed |

**What we can say from this (derived):**

- Even on the *lowest* estimate (95 MLD in, 55 MLD of treatment), **at least ~40 MLD of untreated water enters every day**. That is about 16 Olympic pools a day.
- On the 2026 reported figure (450 MLD), the gap would be **~395 MLD**.
- At 95 MLD the nalas bring in a volume equal to the **whole lake about every 9.5 months** (27.18 Mm³ ÷ 95,000 m³/day ≈ 286 days). At 450 MLD it is about every **2 months**.
- **Data gap:** no verified, current flow measurement for the four nalas was found. Phase 0 of the plan installs flow meters to settle this.

## 3. Water quality vs. standards

CPCB primary water-quality criteria (designated best use):

| Class | Use | DO (mg/L) | BOD (mg/L) | Total coliform (MPN/100 mL) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B | Outdoor bathing | ≥ 5 | ≤ 3 | ≤ 500 |
| C | Drinking source after treatment | ≥ 4 | ≤ 3 | ≤ 5,000 |
| D | Wildlife and fisheries | ≥ 4 | – | – |

Measured in Hussain Sagar:

| Parameter | Measured | When / where | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dissolved oxygen (DO) | **1.28–3.0 mg/L, mean 1.91** | 2016–2020 dataset | IJARIIT paper (WQI study) |
| DO | **Nil (0)** during immersion | Sept 2025; NTR Park Platforms 1 & 2, Lumbini Park | TGPCB via Telangana Today, 5 Oct 2025 |
| BOD | **24–76 mg/L** pre-lockdown → **~8 mg/L** in the 2020 lockdown | 2020 | TSPCB, quoted by Siasat |
| BOD | **94 mg/L** (peak) | Sept 2025, NTR Park Platform 1 | TGPCB via Telangana Today |
| COD | **280 mg/L** (peak) | Sept 2025, NTR Park Platform 1 | TGPCB via Telangana Today |
| COD | **164 mg/L** (peak) | Sept 2026, Necklace Road | TGPCB via Deccan Chronicle / Telangana Today, 30 Oct 2026 |
| Turbidity | **200 NTU** (peak) | Sept 2026, Necklace Road | TGPCB, 30 Oct 2026 |
| TSS | **99 mg/L** (peak) | Sept 2026, Platform 1 opp. NTR Park | TGPCB, 30 Oct 2026 |
| Total coliform | **1,600 MPN/100 mL** at 8 of 9 points | 2023 | Pollution Control Board via Deccan Chronicle, 7 Jun 2023 |
| Faecal coliform | 350 MPN/100 mL (GHMC office, Boat Club) | 2023 | Deccan Chronicle, 7 Jun 2023 |
| Electrical conductivity | 1,693 µS/cm (PVNR Marg) | 2023 | Deccan Chronicle, 7 Jun 2023 |
| Total / faecal coliform | 430 MPN/100 mL (Lumbini Park) | Sept 2025 | TGPCB via Telangana Today |

**Verdict:** typical DO (≈1.9 mg/L) fails even the **Class D fish-survival** threshold (≥ 4 mg/L). Typical BOD (24–76 mg/L) is **8–25×** the bathing limit of 3 mg/L. Coliform at 1,600 MPN is **3.2×** the bathing limit, and 1,600 is the top of the standard MPN test range, so the true count may be higher.

## 4. Latest event: Ganesh immersion, September 2026 (TGPCB)

- Sampling: **11 Sept** (pre), **17, 19, 21, 23 Sept** (during), **28 Sept** (post). Festival day 14 Sept 2026.
- Six sites: Platforms 1 & 2 opposite NTR Park, Lumbini Park, Necklace Road, Lepakshi Handicrafts immersion point, Buddha Statue (mid-lake).
- Turbidity, TSS, COD and BOD rose at **all six** sites. Faecal coliform rose at four.
- Peaks: turbidity **200 NTU**, COD **164 mg/L** (Necklace Road), TSS **99 mg/L** (Platform 1).
- **Not back to pre-immersion levels** at the post-immersion sampling.
- DO: no significant change attributed to immersion (2026). In 2025 it went to **nil** at three sites.

## 6. International references used in the design

| Reference | Key facts | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Switzerland, "4th treatment stage" | Revised Water Protection Act in force **1 Jan 2016**. ~100 (later ~140) of ~700 treatment plants get an extra stage over 20 years. Target **≥ 80% micropollutant removal**. Ozonation and/or activated carbon. Funded by a ~CHF 9/person/year levy | admin.ch / Eawag (2015); McArdell, Eawag (Stowa 2019) |
| Basel, Switzerland | New plant with 4th stage serves ~500,000 people | ICPR |
| Stockholm, Henriksdal | World's largest underground treatment plant, built in rock tunnels. Becoming the world's largest **MBR** (membrane bioreactor). Capacity doubling to **> 500,000 m³/day** for 1.6 M people by 2040. Aim: cut N and P to the Baltic | NIB (2016); GE |
| The Ocean Cleanup Interceptor | 100% solar river barrier, rated 50 t/day. Lesson: **needs flowing water**. Jakarta officials called it unsuitable for low-flow rivers | Eco-Business (2020); Maritime Executive |
| RanMarine WasteShark | Small electric aqua-drone, ~500 kg/day claimed, ~8 h per charge, 160 L basket | RanMarine; RINA |

**Design lesson:** fixed interceptors work where water flows (the nala mouths), so that is where the plant goes. The open lake is nearly still, so the surface needs **mobile** solar boats.
